# Session 2026-08-06 — the text-stream session

Started from Thought 11 (store the dictionary in the table) and Thought 12
(treat the CSV as a text file). Eight numbered tests, TEST-8 through TEST-16.
Three subagents: two research, one design.

**Resource budget, set mid-session: 2 threads and 3 GB, agents included.**
Enforced by a watchdog (`scratchpad/watchdog.py`) rather than by memory; it
killed two runs that crossed the ceiling and one that did not — see "the
watchdog's own bug" below.

## The two things worth carrying forward

**1. LAW 3 — the win is the reordering, and only the reordering.** Recorded in
`LAWS.md`. On the seven tables where reordering does not fire, a byte-level
context model with no idea the file is a table gets within 1–5% of us.

**2. Cross-column redundancy is the answer to LAW 3.** It is the one structure
a general compressor structurally cannot see, and it is worth **twice as much
to us as to xz** — 19.3% vs 9.9% — precisely because our columns are
compressed apart and the duplicate is never adjacent to its source.

## The competitor-set hole

All 17 competitors in `benchmarks/measure_one.py` were LZ-family, Huffman or
block-sort. **No PPM or context-mixing model had ever been measured against.**
PPMd was added this session (`ppmd_rows`, orders 6/12/16, via temp files —
7-Zip answers `E_NOTIMPL` for stdin→stdout on `.7z`). The 500-dataset headline
survives (TEST-11) but should be quoted as *"17 LZ-family and columnar
competitors"* for any sweep run before today.

External check worth acting on: **kanzi `-l9` is −25.3% vs xz on this corpus
and beats Polypress on 7 of 13 tables** (research agent, 10 MB truncations,
round-trip exact). Those seven are our documented weak genre. TEST-14 is
written and queued to verify it under this repo's own harness.

## Negative results, so nobody pays for them twice

- **Word-level Huffman / word-replacing transforms are dead on this data.**
  Three independent measurements: TEST-12 (3.48x), TEST-3 (1.216x), the
  research agent's own WRT implementation (−1.02% aggregate, worse on 6 of 13,
  and *negative* in front of a context mixer). Government-table strings are
  near-duplicates of each other, which is LZ77's strength and a word
  dictionary's blind spot.
- **Trained dictionaries (`zstd --train`) lose 12%** on whole files. They are a
  65% win only at 4 KB chunks — `stream.py` territory, not archival.
- **"Ditto" sigils for cells equal to the row above**: 46.6% of cells qualify
  and it still loses (1.047x), because it destroys the long cross-row match xz
  was already making. Fourth time this repo has hit that shape.
- **A transform in front of a context model is worse than either alone.**
  Measured three ways this session.

## Bugs found, and what they repeat

- **The all-or-nothing gate, walked into while quoting it.** TEST-15 v1
  required every row of a column to share a template and found **nothing on the
  entire corpus**; `seattle_fire911.report_location` matches on 39,922 of
  40,000 rows and is blank on 78. Modal template + row exceptions → 0 tables
  became 3.
- **Derived columns chain and cycle** (TEST-15) — same shape `turbo` fixed in
  `12fb00d`.
- **A partial recipe silently rebuilt wrong cells** (TEST-15) — no error, just
  wrong data. Caught only by cell-by-cell comparison. This is why invariant 4
  exists.
- **Canonical Huffman must advance the code for empty lengths** (TEST-12) —
  skipping them mis-assigns every code after the first gap.
- **The watchdog's own bug:** it used macOS "Pages free", which is near zero by
  design, and killed a 0.74 GB job on an idle machine with 4.9 GB sitting in
  reclaimable inactive pages. Available = free + speculative + inactive +
  purgeable, plus the kernel's own pressure level.
