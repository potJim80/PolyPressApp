SUMMARY -- 2026-08-04.  Does splitting the xz finish cost anything at scale?

Read the "vs one" column only. Absolute bytes are NOT comparable across dict
sizes: a shrunken window makes everything compress worse, and that is an
artefact of the simulation, not a prediction about a real large table. The
ratio is what carries over; the absolute context does not.

PERCOL penalty vs one stream, by column/dictionary ratio:

  ratio     cdc_nndss     nyc_311   chicago_permits
           (16 cols)    (44 cols)      (116 cols)
   ~0.0      +7.26%      +19.12%        +13.37%
   ~0.2      +7.39%      +17.52%        +12.42%
   ~0.3-0.8  +5.92%       +9.67%         +7.74%
   ~1-3      +3.76%       +1.98%         +3.15%
   ~5-33     +3.44%       +0.91%         +1.62%

CONFIRMED, and it is the whole point: the cost of per-column streams collapses
once the dictionary can no longer span a column. nyc_311 goes 19.1% -> 0.9%.
The cross-column reach that `pool` exploits is a SMALL-TABLE effect; at scale
it is already gone and splitting forfeits nothing that still existed.

A 50 GB table sits at ratio 6.7 (116 cols) to ~98 (8 cols), i.e. at or past
the right-hand end of this table. Expect percol to cost roughly 1-3% there.
Magnitude is extrapolated -- the simulation matches the ratio, not the volume.

TWO RESULTS THAT WERE NOT PREDICTED:

1. BYKIND IS FREE AT EVERY RATIO. Two streams -- binary units (intp/dict/
   pair/perm) and text units -- cost between -1.15% and +0.41% everywhere,
   and are NEGATIVE on 5 of 18 points. Splitting binary from text loses no
   cross-column matching worth having, because the matches that pay are
   between columns of the same kind, and those stay in the same stream.

2. PER-STREAM ENTROPY TUNING IS WORTH NOTHING. percol+tune vs percol differs
   by 0.01-0.03% at every one of 18 points; bykind+tune likewise. The theory
   was that one lc/lp/pb across mixed binary and text is a compromise and that
   per-stream settings would recover it. It does not. Drop the theory -- do
   not spend another session on per-column entropy parameters.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE BOUNDED-MEMORY DESIGN: use bykind, not percol. It is free
at all scales AND it is compatible with an external transpose -- walk the
column spill files twice, binary units into one stream and text into the
other, one dictionary live at a time. percol is only worth its 1-3% if column
parallelism or per-column random access is wanted for its own sake.
